How it works

The Visibility Score predicts how visible each brand in an AI-generated answer is to readers, using a model trained in the research paper below.

Try it on an answer

From answer to score

Three steps turn a ChatGPT answer into a visibility score per brand.

1. Read the answer

ChatGPT identifies every brand in the response, which passages describe it, whether there is a recommendation section, how strongly it endorses brands, and the response format.

2. Measure the structure

From that, the page computes each brand's rank of first mention, its share of the text, its share and rank within the recommendation, and the number of competing brands.

3. Predict visibility

A gradient-boosted tree model (XGBoost), trained on visibility ratings from three online experiments, turns these features into a score from 1 (not at all visible) to 7 (extremely visible).

The research paper

Beyond Ranks: Measuring Brand Visibility in LLM Responses

Manuscript under review at Marketing Science

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly shaping how consumers discover brands, yet existing visibility measures primarily capture whether and how frequently brands appear across collections of responses. They provide limited insight into how prominently consumers perceive a brand within an individual response. We introduce within-response brand visibility as a distinct consumer-facing construct and examine how it emerges from the architecture of LLM-generated responses. Study 1 analyzes 100,800 responses from four major LLM families across 200 products and services. The results reveal substantial heterogeneity in brand rank, textual allocation, response format, competitive scope, and recommendation structure, with recommendation-oriented guidance appearing in 79.8% of responses. Two main randomized online experiments (N = 2,415) subsequently examine how these structural characteristics shape perceived visibility and downstream brand exploration. Rank and textual prominence matter, but adding recommendation-related features produces the largest incremental improvement in out-of-sample prediction. Brands receiving recommendation-related emphasis remain more visible even when they occupy less favorable positions. An extensive multi-study robustness battery (N = 2,049), including additional online experiments and laboratory replications, demonstrates that this advantage generalizes across response formats, recommendation framings, competitive-set sizes, consumption domains, participant populations, and data-collection settings. In Study 2B, providers perceived as more visible are substantially more likely to be explored and to be clicked earlier. These findings distinguish cross-response brand presence from within-response brand visibility and identify recommendation as a multidimensional evaluative architecture through which LLMs allocate consumer attention among competing brands.
